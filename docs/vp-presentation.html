Recommendation

Keep Power Apps during a 90-day pilot.

You pay $250K/year for three internal apps and expect at least ten more. That makes owned code worth testing. A two-hour prototype cannot tell us whether it is cheaper to operate.

What I would do

Build two new apps on a shared foundation while the existing apps stay on Power Apps. Include one ordinary queue and one with a difficult integration.

What would change the recommendation

Repeatable delivery, an approved security and operations model, and a lower three-year cost that includes engineers' time. Check the actual license terms before counting savings.

Three explicit apps and one shared set of controls.

The workbench includes KYC review, refund approvals, and feature-flag administration. The apps share a React shell, an Express API, and SQLite for the demo.

In each app

Domain-specific routes and permissions; state changes require a reason and expected version. The change and its audit event commit in one transaction.

For app 4

A scaffolder produces a service, workspace, manifest, and starter test. A repository check catches missing permissions and test coverage. Engineers still implement and review the app.

Economics / what to measure5 of 6
Build versus buy

$250K/year is a baseline, not a savings estimate.

The license only becomes avoidable if the team can retire enough seats or capacity under its contract. Building ten more apps also consumes engineering time that could go to the core product.

Price the owned path

Initial platform work + per-app delivery + integrations + security review + hosting + maintenance and on-call + migration.

Price the Power Apps path

Renewal and incremental licensing + maker and admin time + custom extensions + support. Ask what spend disappears as apps move.

Compare over three years: money spent, engineer-hours, time to ship, incidents, and who can make a change without an engineer. Use contract and pilot data rather than an assumed per-app savings figure.

The pilot should include time from the engineers who build and operate the foundation.05 / 06
Next step / decision gate6 of 6
90-day pilot

Keep the current apps in place. Test two new ones.

  1. Weeks 1–2Inventory app usage, license terms, integrations, current support load, and security requirements.
  2. Weeks 2–5Add SSO and group mapping, secrets, deployment, monitoring, backup, review, and an on-call owner to the foundation.
  3. Weeks 5–10Ship a queue app and an integration-heavy app with Devin helping write code and tests. Track delivery and support time.
  4. Weeks 11–13Review security and reliability; compare full three-year costs. Migrate only if the team can own operations and the numbers hold up.

If the pilot fails, retain Power Apps for standard apps. Devin can still help with custom extensions, integrations, tests, and tools that exceed the platform's limits.
